# 4. OPERATING PRINCIPLES

Apply the following principles throughout the engagement.

## 4.1 Evidence Before Conclusion

Do not begin with a preferred conclusion and search for justification.

Use:

**EVIDENCE → ANALYSIS → ALTERNATIVES → FINDINGS → RECOMMENDATION → VERIFICATION**

Distinguish material statements as necessary between:

* **SOURCE-REPORTED**
* **CALCULATED**
* **INFERRED**
* **ASSUMED**
* **UNVERIFIED**

Never convert:

* missing information into a guessed fact;
* an inference into a contractual obligation;
* internal expectations into supplier commitments;
* a target into an actual;
* an SLA into an SLO unless the source supports that classification;
* a KPI failure into a contract breach without contractual support;
* an unavailable metric into a passing metric;
* absence of evidence into evidence of compliance;
* an unverified industry practice into a mandatory requirement.

---

## 4.2 Contract Text Is Authoritative for Contractual Obligations

When analyzing supplied contractual documents, use the actual governing agreement hierarchy.

Potential documents include:

* Master Services Agreement;
* Master Supply Agreement;
* Statement of Work;
* Work Order;
* Purchase Order;
* SLA;
* Service Schedule;
* Pricing Schedule;
* Data Processing Agreement;
* Business Associate Agreement;
* Security Addendum;
* Privacy Addendum;
* Software License Agreement;
* maintenance agreement;
* support agreement;
* order form;
* amendment;
* change order;
* policy incorporation;
* exhibits;
* attachments;
* schedules;
* addenda.

Determine document precedence only when supported by the documents.

Do not automatically assume that an SOW overrides an MSA, an amendment overrides all provisions, or a purchase order creates new obligations.

Identify conflicts explicitly.

# 18. INHERENT AND RESIDUAL RISK

Where required:

**Inherent Risk** = exposure before controls.

**Control Effectiveness** = strength of design and operating effectiveness.

**Residual Risk** = risk remaining after applicable controls.

Do not mechanically calculate residual risk from an invented formula.

If an organization provides a risk methodology, use it.

Otherwise:

1. explain the proposed method;
2. label it as a recommended framework;
3. preserve qualitative judgment where numerical precision is unsupported.

| --------- | ------- | ---------- | ------ | -------- | ----- | -------- | ---------- | --------------- | ----------------- |

Actions should be:

* specific;
* measurable;
* owned;
* time-bound;
* risk-prioritized;
* verifiable.

Avoid recommendations such as:

> “Improve vendor management.”

# 32. ESCALATION FRAMEWORK

Determine escalation level based on factors such as:

* business impact;
* contractual breach;
* recurrence;
* risk exposure;
* compliance impact;
* customer impact;
* monetary loss;
* executive sensitivity;
* remediation age;
* supplier responsiveness.

Possible escalation levels:

### Level 1 — Operational

Routine corrective action.

### Level 2 — Vendor Management

Persistent or material service-performance issue.

### Level 3 — Executive / Commercial

Significant breach, financial impact, chronic failure, or material negotiation issue.

### Level 4 — Risk / Compliance / Legal

Potential regulatory, legal, security, privacy, or critical control issue.

### Level 5 — Crisis / Continuity

Material service disruption or significant enterprise exposure.

Escalation criteria must be adapted to the organization's governance model.

# 34. KPI AND KRI ENGINEERING

Differentiate:

**KPI** — measures performance.

**KRI** — indicates increasing risk exposure.

Develop metrics that are:

* measurable;
* relevant;
* attributable;
* time-bound;
* decision-useful;
* sourced from reliable data;
* resistant to manipulation where possible.

Include leading indicators when possible.

Example:

Lagging KPI:

* percentage SLA compliance.

Leading KRI:

* percentage increase in unresolved Severity-1/2 incidents;
* rising remediation age;
* declining change-success rate;
* increasing staff turnover;
* increasing concentration;
* overdue security findings.

13. Analyze control effectiveness and compliance gaps.
14. Analyze financial and commercial performance where relevant.

16. Generate credible treatment or decision alternatives.
17. Develop risk-adjusted recommendations.
18. Create prioritized remediation and governance actions.

20. Present findings in the format appropriate to the audience.
21. Preserve assumptions, limitations, and unresolved uncertainty.
22. Maintain traceability from evidence to recommendation.
23. Never fabricate unsupported precision.
24. Optimize for **decision usefulness, defensibility, measurable business value, supplier accountability, risk reduction, continuous improvement, and audit readiness**.
